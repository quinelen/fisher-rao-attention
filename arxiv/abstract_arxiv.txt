We propose attention in which queries and keys are probability distributions and the score is their exact Fisher-Rao geodesic distance. Once the family is chosen, invariance under sufficient statistics fixes the metric up to scale: categorical tokens give a spherical kernel, isotropic Gaussian tokens a hyperbolic one, and Euclidean distance attention is the flat Gaussian location family. The Gaussian kernel is hyperbolic distance attention in half-space coordinates; its exact distance costs one matrix multiplication plus an elementwise arccosh (2.3-2.8x a dot-product module on CPU). On Dyck-2 with matched parameters and a fixed budget, accuracy beyond the training length ranks dot-product 85.8% < spherical 86.6% < hyperbolic 88.4%, with the same margin within it, so the kernel learns nested structure better rather than extrapolating separately; the contrast holds over 20 paired seeds (+2.10 pp, 95% CI 1.74-2.46), is larger in one 19M-parameter configuration (+4.7 pp, 3 seeds) and persists with rotary positions (+1.82 pp, n=20, pre-registered). With Gaussian tokens fixed, it beats Wasserstein-Bures and KL kernels, and a variant without per-token scales keeps most of the gain: the evidence points to the geodesic kernel, not yet separating curvature from slow score growth. With untrained absolute positions, a norm-capped variant degrades less than dot-product attention beyond the training length on code and English prose (pre-registered), but not on Spanish clinical text; these small models are near character-bigram quality. Pre-registered probes find second-layer query scales narrowing and key scales widening with nesting depth, a decodable code whose removal from the query scales costs 0.15 pp beyond the training length (0.41 pp within it). A reimplementation of curvature-adaptive routing (CAT) shows no significant difference. Negative results are reported alongside the positive ones.

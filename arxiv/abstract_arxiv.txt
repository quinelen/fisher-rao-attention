Standard attention scores tokens by inner products in a flat space. We propose attention in which queries and keys are probability distributions and similarity is the exact Fisher-Rao geodesic distance on the statistical manifold: the distributional family is a design choice, but once chosen, Chentsov's theorem fixes the metric. Categorical tokens yield a spherical kernel (K=+1/4), isotropic Gaussian tokens a genuinely hyperbolic one (K<0), and Euclidean distance attention is the Gaussian family's local flat regime. The exact hyperbolic geodesic reduces to one matrix multiplication plus an elementwise transcendental on an LxL matrix (2.3x a standard attention module). On Dyck-2 length extrapolation with matched parameters, accuracy ranks Euclidean 85.8% < spherical 86.6% < hyperbolic 88.4%; the contrast holds over 20 paired seeds (+2.10 pp, p=1.9e-10), widens at 46x scale (+4.7 pp, 19M parameters), and persists with relative (rotary) positions in both arms (+1.82 pp, n=20, p=6e-5). With Gaussian tokens held fixed, the Fisher-Rao kernel beats both a Wasserstein-Bures kernel (+1.52 pp) and a KL (Bregman) kernel sharing its local information geometry (+1.11 pp, p=0.010), attributing the gain to the global hyperbolic geometry; a positive-definite Laplacian variant keeps most of it. Above a width threshold it shows no significant accuracy loss versus Euclidean attention at twice the width, with 3.8-3.9x fewer parameters. After two pre-registered failures on code, a one-line mu-norm cap let the kernel pass pre-registered far-extrapolation endpoints on real Python source (n=20, p=0.0037), and the same kernel passed at first attempt on unseen technical English prose (n=10, p=0.0002), while losing in distribution as predicted. Against a reimplementation of the curvature-adaptive incumbent CAT it ties (88.4% vs 88.1%, p=0.53) at lower cost. Pre-specified negative results bound the claims.
